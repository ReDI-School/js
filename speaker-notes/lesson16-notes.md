## Berlin Underground Guide – Introduction
- "Berlin Underground Guide" is a website designed to showcase Berlin's hidden underground music venues, mainly located in basements
- It is a digital guide that combines location-based data and interactive maps
- It also provides details about each venue: descriptions, images and websites
- It uses a map, pins and JavaScript techniques, plus the CSS and HTML styling from the previous semester at ReDI School

## Berlin Underground Guide – Purpose & Motivation
- The motivation was to combine a passion for underground music with web development
- Berlin is famous for its underground music scene
- The map-based guide helps both locals and visitors discover unique places they might not find otherwise
- The project showcases what was learned throughout the semester: HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Leaflet for mapping

## Berlin Underground Guide – Key features
1. Interactive map: the website uses the Leaflet.js library to display an interactive map of Berlin with markers for the different venues
2. Venue modals: clicking a venue on the map opens a modal with an image, description, website link and photo credit (when applicable)
3. Dark/light mode: a button toggles between dark and light themes, for a personalized user experience
4. About modal: an informational popup about the website itself and its creator

## Berlin Underground Guide – Technology stack
- HTML5: the structure and content of the web pages
- CSS3: styling, including responsive design and theme switching
- JavaScript: dynamic behavior – modals, map markers and light/dark mode toggling
- Leaflet.js: creating the interactive map
- Google Fonts: for a modern, readable design
- Flickr.com: used to host the pictures

## Berlin Underground Guide – Challenges & Solutions
- Integrating the interactive map with venue data
- Designing modals to display the venue information neatly
- Ensuring that both dark and light modes were compatible
- Styling the website for mobile responsiveness
- The source material lists only the challenges, not the solutions – ask the class how they would solve them, or ask the original author

## Online Demo Day – structure
- **Introduction (1 minute)**
  - Briefly describe the project and its importance
  - Explain the need it addresses and the problem it solves
  - Identify the audience it benefits
- **Core of the presentation**
  - Focus on the additional research conducted by the team
  - Ensure classmates learn something new
- **Demo**
  - Include a practical demonstration, case study, or explanation of how the project applies in the real world
  - Provide specific examples for better understanding
- **Short conclusions (1 minute)**
  - Reflect on how the project could be expanded with more time
  - Share challenges faced as a team and how they were resolved
  - Highlight lessons learned throughout the project process

## KISS
- Keep it Short and Simple
- Keep it structured
- Rehearse and timebox yourself
- Know your audience

## The 6x6 rule
- Each slide should contain no more than 6 words per line and no more than 6 lines per slide
- Use visuals (images, icons, charts) to spice up your presentation and tell a story
- Keep the font size large enough to be easily readable (at least 14-20 pt)
- Use bullet points – don't read off your slides. No chunky paragraphs of text

## Story format
- Engage your audience with memorable examples
- Set the scene, the characters and the task at hand
- There should be a start, a middle and an end
- Story arc: intro → development → key insight → resolution → wrap-up
- Introduce some drama and tie it up in the conclusion
- Draw from personal stories and experiences; add some humor/empathy to make a connection with the audience
