# Practice For If Statements

We had such a wonderful time looking at the skies together in class, so let’s keep that momentum rolling! Mother Nature throws all kinds of surprises our way, and just like choosing the right outfit or drink, making smart decisions is all about knowing which rule to pull out of your toolkit.

Below are 4 fresh weather scenarios. You have 4 different decision structures to choose from:


Single if
if - else
if - else if
if - else if - else


:dart: Rule for this homework: Each of these 4 structures will be used exactly once. Read each scenario and choose the best structure for the job and submit your code solutions.

:seedling: EXERCISE 1


Weather Scenario: You check the morning weather forecast.
The Problem: You have a specific rule: “If the temperature drops below 0°C, bring your potted plants indoors.” If it is warmer than that, you don’t need to do anything special; you just leave the plants where they are.
Your Task: Which of the 4 structures should you use here, and why do you not need a second option for the warm weather?


:jeans: EXERCISE 2


Weather Scenario: Opening your closet to choose what to wear on your lower body based on the thermometer.
The Problem: You have this rule: “If the temperature is below 20°C, wear long trousers. Otherwise, wear shorts.”
Hint: Remember, you would not put on both trousers and shorts at the same time—you must choose one or the other.
Your Task: Which of the 4 structures fits this strict, either/or choice?


:coat: EXERCISE 3


Weather Scenario: Checking the thermometer before grabbing a coat from the hall closet.
The Problem: You have these specific rules for extra layers: “If the temperature is below 0°C, wear your heavy down parka. If the temperature is between 0°C and 21°C, wear your medium jacket.”
Hint: You check the freezing temperature first. If it is a chilly 15°C, you catch the second rule and grab your medium jacket. If it is a warm 25°C summer day, neither condition matches, and you simply walk out without a jacket—no default else is needed because normal warm weather requires no coat.
Your Task: Which of the 4 structures should you use to check these specific temperature thresholds sequentially without needing a catch-all at the end?


:beverage_box: EXERCISE 4


Weather Scenario: Deciding what afternoon drink to serve your friends based on the thermometer.
The Problem: You want to categorize the temperature into three clear brackets:
Above 30°C -> “Serve iced lemonade”
Between 20°C and 30°C -> “Serve cool water”
Anything else -> “Serve hot tea”

Your Task: Which of the 4 structures ensures that every single temperature on earth gets a drink assigned to it without leaving any gaps?
